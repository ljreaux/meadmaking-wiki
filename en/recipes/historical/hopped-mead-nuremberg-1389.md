## Hopped Red Mead (Nuremberg, c. 1389)

(translated by u/ralfv)

*New to these? Read the [introduction](/recipes/historical) first: it explains what is left out on purpose and what the yeast of the time could do.*

**Source:** Nuremberg, Germanisches Nationalmuseum, Hs 3227a, c. 1389 (the miscellany best known for the oldest Liechtenauer fencing text), recipe 1.

**Language:** Middle High German

**Style:** Hopped mead; caramelized honey added for colour

### Translation

If you want to boil good mead, take three parts water and one part honey and let it boil well. And when it is well boiled, take two *viertail* or more, according to how much mead you are boiling, and carry that into a heated room and set it behind the stove, and let it stand until it gets a little cap; that is then called a *tampfel*.

After that put hops into the other pots and let it boil well, and stir it constantly and very hard, and do not go away from it, or the mead will catch and turn burnt. And when it is well boiled, put it in a cask and pour the *tampfel* into it, and let them work through one another.

Item, for the colour take clear honey and boil it very well, and stir it constantly with a piece of wood. And when the colour is well boiled, test it: take a little board and drip it on it. If the drops stand and are hard, it is good. And when it is fine and red, pour it, hot as it is, into the mead. Then let it stand eight or fourteen days. Then you have good mead.

### Notes

- ***tampfel*:** the warm-room portion that gets a "cap" of foam: a starter that begins fermenting on its own and is then pitched into the main batch. The word is otherwise unknown.
- ***viertail*:** a vessel or measure called a "quarter", not a quarter of the batch.
- **Colour:** the honey is boiled to hard-crack stage (drops set hard on a board) and red, i.e. caramelized, as in a bochet.
- **Ratio:** 3 parts water to 1 part honey. No hop quantity is given.
