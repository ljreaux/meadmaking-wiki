## Juniper and Zedoary Small Mead (Master Hans, 1460)

(translated by u/ralfv)

*New to these? Read the [introduction](/recipes/historical) first: it explains what is left out on purpose and what the yeast of the time could do.*

**Source:** Cookbook of Master Hans, cook to Count Ulrich V of Württemberg, 1460. Basel, University Library, Cod. AN V 12, recipe 163.

**Language:** Early New High German (Alemannic-Swabian)

**Style:** Metheglin, light (honey or sugar)

### Translation

*Of a good drink.*

Item, a good drink for half a *fuder*. Make it like this. Take three *metzen* of juniper berries, or *kranewitt* berries, and boil them until a third has boiled away. Then strain the water off the juniper berries, and then take half a pound of zedoary and half a pound of sugar or a measure of honey, and put that into the water and boil it in it. Then set it aside and let it cool.

And when it is cold, pour it into a cask that is pitched, and then put a little cut galangal (?) into the cask. And keep back a little of the water, and when it has fermented, top it up with that, and bung it up and let it lie until March. Then it becomes a very good, wholesome water drink.

### Notes

- ***fuder*:** a large wine-cask measure (several hundred litres). ***metzen*:** a dry measure for grain and berries.
- ***Kranewitt*** is the southern German name for juniper; both words mean the same berry.
- **Sugar or honey:** one or the other. Only the honey version is a mead.
- **Galangal:** the manuscript has *gaddaus*, read as a hasty spelling of *galgan*.
- **No yeast is mentioned:** assume the established cellar yeast of a working cask (see the [introduction](/recipes/historical#the-yeast-cellar-yeast-not-wait-and-see)).
