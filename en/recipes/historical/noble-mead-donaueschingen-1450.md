## Noble Mead, Boiled Down by a Third (Lake Constance, c. 1450)

(translated by u/ralfv)

*New to these? Read the [introduction](/recipes/historical) first: it explains what is left out on purpose and what the yeast of the time could do.*

**Source:** House and medical book, Karlsruhe, Badische Landesbibliothek, Cod. Donaueschingen 792, fol. 81v, c. 1450 (Alemannic, western Lake Constance region).

**Language:** Early New High German (Alemannic), Latin heading

**Style:** Traditional

### Translation

*Medonem faciendo* [On making mead].

If you want to make good noble mead, take one *som* of honey and two *som* of water, and boil the som of honey very well, for as long as one can properly skim a pot. And when it begins to boil, stir it with the shovel so that it does not boil over. But if you cannot stop it boiling over that way, pour half a dish of water into it.

And when it is about to boil up, take a dish that is fixed to a stick, and skim it really well and deftly, so that the scum does not boil back in. And skim it until it becomes clear. Then take a stick and dip it into the mead in the kettle, and cut a notch in the stick. And let it boil until a third has boiled away. So from one som of honey you should get two som of mead.

When it has been boiled, pour it into a *binte* and let it get cold. Then pour it into a cask. If mead or cherry wine was in the cask before, rinse it with cold water before closing it. But if wine was in it, heat a kettle of boiling water, pour it into the cask, and leave it in there overnight; and roll the cask about with the water, and afterwards rinse it with cold water. Then you have good mead.

And the scum, too, you should boil down by a third, and strain it, and put it in as well. Then that also becomes good. – *dixit.*

### Notes

- ***som*:** a large trade measure for wine and honey (in the region roughly 130–180 l). Only the ratio matters here: 1 honey to 2 water, boiled down by a third.
- **Notched stick:** the notch marks the starting level, so you can see when a third has boiled away.
- ***binte*:** a large wooden vessel used for filling casks.
- **No yeast is mentioned:** assume the established cellar yeast of a working cask (see the [introduction](/recipes/historical#the-yeast-cellar-yeast-not-wait-and-see)).
