## Rose and Laurel Berry Mead (Marx Rumpolt, 1581)

(translated by u/ralfv)

*New to these? Read the [introduction](/recipes/historical) first: it explains what is left out on purpose and what the yeast of the time could do.*

**Source:** Marx Rumpolt, *Ein new Kochbuch* (A New Cookbook), Frankfurt am Main, 1581. Rumpolt was personal cook to the Elector of Mainz.

**Language:** Early New High German (print)

**Style:** Metheglin (rose, laurel berries); caramelized honey for colour

### Translation

*How one shall make good mead.*

Item, take one measure of honey to six measures of water and boil it on the fire as if it were young beef. And while it boils, throw red flower heads of red roses into it, and skim it well as it comes up; and the fire shall burn bright, without smoke. And when it has boiled, take it off and let it cool.

Meanwhile, for 5 *viertel* of the mead that you have boiled, take one measure of honey; put that in a kettle and let it boil over embers, and stir it on and on until it takes on a red or brown colour, as you want the colour to be. Then take some of the boiled mead and pour it into the honey, stir it well, and pour it all together.

After that take fresh laurel berries that still have their skins, crush them and hang them in it, and let it cool well before you put it in the little cask. And if you want to strengthen it with good spice or with hops, you may well do that too.

### Quantities

Rumpolt uses two units. Despite its name ("quarter"), the *Viertel* is bigger than the *Maß*: it holds four of them, and twenty Viertel make one *Ohm*, the next wine measure up. Frankfurt values (19th century; the size in 1581 is not documented but was probably close):

Unit | Metric | US
---|---|---
1 *Maß* (measure) | about 1.8 l | about ½ gallon (1.9 quarts)
1 *Viertel* = 4 Maß | about 7.2 l | about 1.9 gallons (7.6 quarts)
1 *Ohm* = 20 Viertel = 80 Maß | about 143 l | about 38 gallons

The recipe has two ratios:

Step | In the text | In modern terms
---|---|---
Base mead | 1 Maß honey to 6 Maß water | 1 part honey to 6 parts water, by volume
Colouring honey | 1 Maß honey for every 5 Viertel of mead | about 1.8 l honey per 36 l of mead (½ gallon per 9.5 gallons), so roughly 1 part honey to 20 parts mead

Example for a 5-gallon batch (about 19 l of mead):

Ingredient | Metric | US
---|---|---
Honey for the base mead | about 2.7 l (3.8 kg) | about 0.7 gallon (8.4 lb)
Water | about 16.2 l | about 4.3 gallons
Honey for colouring, boiled dark | about 0.95 l (1.35 kg) | about 1 quart (3 lb)

Honey weights assume about 1.42 kg per litre (11.8 lb per gallon).

### Notes

- **"As if it were young beef":** boil it the way you would boil young beef. No time is given.
- ***viertel*:** read as the Frankfurt cask measure of 4 Maß, not a quarter of a Maß. The claret recipe right after this one boils its honey in half a Maß of wine before adding it to a single Viertel, so the Viertel has to be the bigger unit.
- **Colour:** a separate measure of honey is boiled until red or brown and mixed back in, as in a bochet.
- **Laurel berries:** the berries of bay laurel (*Laurus nobilis*), not the leaves. Rumpolt writes *Lorberbeer* here, and elsewhere in the book *Lorberbletter* for the leaves; "that have skins" and "crush them" also only fit the fruit. Not American bayberry (*Myrica*) and not mountain laurel (*Kalmia*), which is poisonous.
- **Hang them in it:** Rumpolt's neighbouring wine recipes hang such additions in a little bag or cloth.
- **No yeast is mentioned:** assume the established cellar yeast of a working cask (see the [introduction](/recipes/historical#the-yeast-cellar-yeast-not-wait-and-see)).
